Australia's History

From the world's oldest continuous living culture to a modern federation of six states — a story spanning more than 65,000 years.

65,000 Years in the Making

Australia's history is unlike that of any other nation. It begins with Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples — the world's oldest continuous cultures — then passes through European exploration and colonial settlement, and culminates in one of the world's most stable modern democracies.

The First Australians

Aboriginal peoples arrived on the continent at least 65,000 years ago, making theirs the longest continuous culture in human history. They developed sophisticated knowledge of land, season, water, and sky, expressed through Dreaming stories, songlines, art, and ceremony.

More than 250 distinct language groups existed at the time of European contact, each with its own territory, laws, and traditions. Rock art, fish traps, and fire-management practices show thousands of years of careful land stewardship.

Torres Strait Islander peoples, with a distinct culture linked to the islands between Cape York and Papua New Guinea, are the second group of Indigenous Australians.

Exploration & Colonization

Dutch navigator Willem Janszoon made the first recorded European landing in 1606, and the continent was later named "New Holland." In 1770, Captain James Cook charted the east coast and claimed it for Britain.

The First Fleet arrived at Sydney Cove on January 26, 1788, establishing a penal colony. Over the following decades, settlements spread across the continent, often with devastating consequences for Indigenous peoples through dispossession and disease.

Gold rushes from 1851 brought hundreds of thousands of migrants, transforming the colonies' economy, society, and ambitions for self-government.

Ancient Culture

Dreaming stories, songlines, bark painting, dot art, and ceremony carry knowledge across generations — a living heritage, not a relic of the past.

Colonial Era

Convict settlements, free settlers, pastoral expansion, and the gold rush built the colonies — alongside a long and difficult frontier history.

Modern Nation

Federation in 1901 created a nation that has since become a multicultural, prosperous democracy and a key Indo-Pacific power.
