Australian Pop Culture

From the Crocodile Hunter to Hollywood's biggest names, Australia's personalities have shaped entertainment around the globe.

The Faces of a Nation

Australian pop culture is loud, warm, and unmistakably Down Under. No one represents that spirit better than Steve Irwin — the wildlife presenter whose enthusiasm made the whole world fall in love with Australia's animals.

Icon Spotlight · 1962–2006

Steve Irwin

Known to millions as "The Crocodile Hunter," Steve Irwin was a wildlife expert, zookeeper, and television personality from Queensland. With his khaki shirt, broad accent, and fearless energy, he introduced audiences in more than 200 countries to crocodiles, snakes, and other misunderstood creatures.

Steve and his wife Terri ran Australia Zoo on the Sunshine Coast and starred in The Crocodile Hunter, which premiered in 1996. His catchphrases — "Crikey!" and "Danger, danger, danger!" — became part of global pop culture.

He died in 2006 after being struck by a stingray while filming on the Great Barrier Reef. His family and Australia Zoo continue his conservation work through Wildlife Warriors, and he remains one of the most recognized Australians in history.

"I'm a wildlife warrior, and I'm going to fight for the animals until the day I die."

Hugh Jackman

Tony- and Emmy-winning actor best known as Wolverine in the X-Men films and as a celebrated stage performer in Les Misérables and The Greatest Showman.

Nicole Kidman

Academy Award-winning actress from Sydney and one of Australia's most acclaimed exports, known for Moulin Rouge!, The Hours, and Big Little Lies.

Kylie Minogue

Pop icon and Grammy winner whose career began on Australian television and grew into a global music phenomenon with hits such as "Can't Get You Out of My Head."

AC/DC

Formed in Sydney in 1973, AC/DC became one of the best-selling rock bands of all time with anthems such as "Highway to Hell" and "Thunderstruck."

Chris Hemsworth

Born in Melbourne, Hemsworth rose to fame as Thor in the Marvel Cinematic Universe and is one of Hollywood's most bankable leading men.

Margot Robbie

Queensland-born actress and producer who was Oscar-nominated for I, Tonya and is famous worldwide as Harley Quinn and Barbie.

Why it matters: Australian performers, musicians, and personalities have carried the country's relaxed, irreverent character into global entertainment — while using their reach to support conservation, the arts, and Australian communities.

Screen & Music

Australia punches far above its weight in film and music. Actors from Sydney and Melbourne are mainstays of Hollywood, while Australian bands and singers have topped charts on every continent.

Australian cinema has produced international classics including Mad Max, Crocodile Dundee, and Picnic at Hanging Rock.

Sporting Heroes

Sport is central to Australian identity. Cricket legends such as Don Bradman, swimmers such as Ian Thorpe and Emma McKeon, and tennis champions from Rod Laver to Ash Barty are national heroes.

Major events such as the Australian Open, the Ashes, and the AFL Grand Final draw millions of viewers each year.
